# Assumptions and simplifications

Every proxy and simplification gets logged here.

| # | Stage | Assumption | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Setup | Ruff and pytest settings added to pyproject.toml | The pasted instruction for extra pyproject entries was cut off |
| 2 | A1 | `Price` is treated as MRP and `DiscountPrice` as the selling price | `DiscountPrice` is never above `Price` (equal on 319 rows, lower on 7,889). Not confirmed by the dataset documentation |
| 3 | A1 | Discount % = (MRP - selling price) / MRP. Prices are one BigBasket snapshot, not a time series | Only one price per product is available |
| 4 | A1 | **Category map v1** (`config/category_map.csv`) is keyed on `SubCategory` (unique across categories) and covers 35 subcategories in 11 target categories. Unmapped subcategories keep `target_category` empty | First proposal, to be reviewed. Premium and organic subcategories (olive oil, organic flours, gourmet tea, ...) are deliberately left out so the ladders reflect mass-market packs |
| 5 | A1 | `edible_oil_ghee` (11 rows) and `noodles_pasta` (25 rows) are thin, so their ladders will be weak | Few mass-market SKUs on BigBasket in these subcategories |
| 6 | A1 | Brand cleaning: trim spaces; ALL-CAPS names of 5+ letters become Title Case; fully lowercase names get capitals; short caps names (DP, MTR, VLCC) and names with symbols are left as they are; manual overrides in `config/brand_map.csv` | No case-variant duplicates exist in the data, so this is for consistent display only. Short caps names are probably acronyms |
| 7 | A1 | Exact duplicates (same name, brand, quantity, MRP and selling price) are dropped (52 rows). Same name/brand/quantity with different prices are kept and flagged `dup_listing` (103 rows) | Different prices suggest separate listings or snapshots |
| 8 | A1 | Pack sizes are converted to grams, millilitres or pieces. `1 kg` = 1,000 g, `1 L` = 1,000 ml (no density conversion between g and ml) | Keeps weights and volumes separate, so detergent powder (g) and liquid (ml) are not compared |
| 9 | A1 | Count-based packs (pcs, tablets, wipes, sachets, "set of N", "10's pack") get a **price per piece** (e.g. Rs 120 for 12 pcs = Rs 10 each) and no price per 100 g | There is no weight to divide by |
| 10 | A1 | Combos (`Combo`, `bb Combo`) stay in the file with `is_combo = True` and no unit price. They are excluded from price ladders in A2 | They bundle different products, so their total weight is not one comparable pack |
| 11 | A1 | `"500 ml each"` is read as one 500 ml unit (noted `each`). `"120 g 12 pcs"` is read by its weight part (noted `trailing_text`) | The text is ambiguous; this is the simplest reading |
| 12 | A1 | `"25 pcs + 12 pcs"` sums the parts. `"1.3 g & 12 ml"` and mixed units are left unparsed | Do not guess when units differ |
| 13 | A1 | If Quantity cannot be read, we try the product name, but only when it contains exactly one size. Lengths (cm, mm, inch), mg and "kit"/"products" are not parsed | Nothing in the target food/household categories needed this fallback (0 rows used it) |
| 14 | A1 | Unparsed rows (18, all non-food) stay in `products_clean.parquet` with `parse_status = unparsed` and are also listed in `data/interim/products_unparsed.csv` | Nothing is silently dropped except exact duplicates |
| 15 | A2 | Ladders use the **selling price** (after discount), grouped by brand x SubCategory x unit (g or ml). Products of one brand and subcategory with the same pack size are collapsed to their median | One comparable price per rung. Switched from brand-only ladders because those mixed unlike products |
| 16 | A2 | Even brand x subcategory ladders can mix product lines (e.g. Cadbury cream biscuits of different types), so 32 of 247 steps still have a larger pack costing fewer rupees. Treat single ladders as indicative; medians across ladders are more reliable | No product-line field exists in the data |
| 17 | A2 | Pack premium = price per 100 g/ml of smallest pack / largest pack, needs 2+ distinct pack sizes (`min_packs_per_ladder`). Category figure = median across ladders (brand x subcategory), so one outlier (e.g. a 2 g premium tea sample, 94x) does not move it | Median is robust to outliers; the ladder-level file keeps the outliers visible |
| 18 | A2 | Ladder gap = size ratio of neighbouring packs (bigger / smaller) above `ladder_gap_ratio` (2.5). The price ratio and rupee jump are saved next to it. `ladder_gaps.csv` holds **all** steps with an `is_gap` flag | Size ratio is the plain reading of "gap between pack sizes"; the rupee jump shows what a shopper must find to step up |
| 19 | A2 | Entry price = cheapest selling price per brand x category over all non-combo products, any pack type | Entry price is about rupees, not grams |
| 20 | A2 | Count packs (9 rows in target categories) and combos (53) are excluded from ladders and premiums | No weight or volume to compare |
| 21 | A2 | Some categories have only 2-4 ladders (laundry detergent g: 2, edible oil: 2). Treat their premium as indicative | Small sample |
| 22 | A3, A5 | **Default spend is state-level.** Monthly spend per person comes from HCES by state x rural/urban, blended for each district with its urban population share, so districts in one state differ only through that share. An **exploratory district-level version** also exists (`make a5-district`, columns `spend_pc_<category>_district`): district spend is used only where the district had at least 100 sampled households (631 of 706 districts), otherwise the state value. It writes separate `*_district_exploratory` files | The HCES data note says estimates below State/UT level may be inaccurate and users should refrain from making them. The exploratory version is for comparison only. Compared with the default, only 4 of 44 pack/price suggestions differ, but relative scores move a lot in some cells (up to 64 points), so the ranking is sensitive to the spend level |
| 23 | A3 | District population = sum(household size x `mult`) / 100 from HCES weights. This gives 1.21 billion for India, about 13% below official projections, so it is a RELATIVE size. Cross-check against Census 2011 on 606 districts with unique names: rank correlation 0.91, median ratio 0.95 | Weights are designed for state estimates; the check shows district sums still track Census well |
| 24 | A3 | NFHS-5 districts created after the HCES frame are mapped to their parent HCES district (`config/district_name_map.csv`) and **share that district's HCES population equally** (e.g. Hapur and Ghaziabad each get half of Ghaziabad). Parents checked against public sources: Kra Daadi (Kurung Kumey), Longding (Tirap), Namsai (Lohit, inferred from Lohit's history), Hapur (Ghaziabad), Sambhal (Moradabad), Shamli (Muzaffarnagar), Amethi (Sultanpur), Charkhi Dadri (Bhiwani), Agar Malwa (Shajapur), Palghar (Thane), Bageshwar (Almora). Meghalaya splits follow the district names (North Garo Hills from East Garo Hills, South West Garo Hills from West Garo Hills, South West Khasi Hills from West Khasi Hills, East and West Jaintia Hills from Jaintia Hills). Equal sharing is a simplification: the real split is unknown. 30 districts share this way | Keeps state totals unchanged and uses the HCES weights, as requested |
| 25 | A3 | District names are matched within each state: hand-made overrides (`config/district_name_map.csv`, 40 rows: 24 spelling fixes and 16 parent mappings), then exact match on the normalised name, then the same with spaces removed. Fuzzy matches are only suggested. NFHS state names are mapped to HCES spellings by `config/state_name_map.csv` (HCES has typos such as "Uttar Prdesh") | A wrong automatic match would silently corrupt features. Ambiguous cases are left unmatched on purpose |
| 26 | A3 | HCES district names come from parsing Appendix I of the Vol I PDF (695 rows, all 692 district codes in the data are named). Some names carry PDF line-break artefacts ("Sou th Twenty Four Parganas") | Needed because the data has only numeric district codes |
| 27 | A3 | HCES item codes are mapped to our categories in `config/hces_item_map.csv`: rice (102), atta (108, maida 110, suji 111), oils and ghee (181-184, 095, 164), spices (dry chillies, turmeric, cumin, coriander, curry powder, pepper, other), tea (cups and leaf), biscuits (013), namkeen/chips (292, 293), noodles (015), detergent (466), toilet soap/body-wash/hand-wash/face-wash (450), shampoo (453). PDS and free items are excluded. HCES categories are broader or narrower than the BigBasket ones (noodles has no separate pasta code; personal wash includes face-wash) | Item codes read from the Vol II questionnaire |
| 28 | A3 | 7-day recall values are scaled by 30/7 to a month; 30-day items are used as they are. Spend per person = sum(value x `mult`) / sum(household size x `mult`) | Recall periods differ by section (5.x: 30 days, 6.x and 7.x: 7 days, 9.x: 30 days) |
| 29 | A3 | HCES column meanings (e.g. `b5pt1q6` = total consumption value) are taken from the column ORDER matching the layout workbook, not from named headers. The India population check (1.21 billion) supports the household-size and weight mapping, but item value columns were not separately verified against published totals | Files use coded column names |
| 30 | A3 | NFHS-5 indicators are matched by a text fragment of the column header (12 indicators, see config). "*" (suppressed, small sample) is read as missing; none of the 12 chosen indicators has missing values | Headers carry glued-on footnote numbers |
| 31 | A3 | Census 2011 is used only as a cross-check (640 districts on 2011 boundaries). The first NFHS file given was a single fact sheet and was replaced by the district workbook | |
| 32 | A4 | Clustering uses the 12 NFHS-5 indicators plus urban share (13 features). Spend is not used because it is state-level. PCA keeps 85% of variance (7 components), k chosen 4-8 by silhouette (k = 4, silhouette 0.22), seed 42 | Silhouette is low, so clusters are a rough development gradient rather than sharp groups |
| 33 | A4 | Sensitivity: ARI >= 0.7 versus baseline counts as stable. k = 5 (0.48) and dropping drinking water (0.40), women's literacy (0.54), women's BMI (0.49) or female schooling (0.62) change the clusters materially | Rule of thumb, not a statistical test. Cluster 3 (59 districts) in particular depends on the drinking-water indicator |
| 34 | A5 | Opportunity index = (benchmark cluster spend per person - cluster spend per person) x cluster population; benchmark = highest-spend cluster in that category. A RELATIVE score, not rupees of sales. `relative_score` rescales it to 0-100 within a category | Public data has no sales. Because it multiplies by population, the biggest cluster by population (cluster 2) tops most categories |
| 35 | A5 | Recommendation rules: a cluster below 80% of the benchmark spend is "lower spend". Pack size = the typical smallest pack (lower-spend) or a pack inside the typical flagged ladder gap if the category has one, else the smallest pack; sizes snap to common retail sizes. **Price point = what brands charge for packs within 1.5x of that size** (25th percentile for lower-spend clusters, median otherwise), falling back to unit price x pack size, or to brands' entry prices when a category has no weight/volume ladder, so price and pack agree. `confidence` is "low" when the category has under 4 ladders or no direct price evidence | Simple, transparent rules. Still directions, not a price list; edible oil, noodles and others have thin evidence |
| 36 | A3 | The compiled NFHS-5 workbook has a copy error: its Mizoram "Chandel" row is an exact duplicate of Manipur's Chandel (847 households, identical indicators). It is dropped (706 districts remain), and Mizoram's Saiha has no NFHS row | The values are not Mizoram's |
| 37 | A3 | 5 districts remain unmatched and get an imputed population (state population not covered by matched districts, shared equally; else the state's median district): Siang (parents West Siang and East Siang, not a single one), Delhi's New Delhi, Shahdara and South East (parents not established), and Mumbai (HCES has only Mumbai Suburban) | No single, verified parent |
| 38 | A4 | Cluster numbers are arbitrary in KMeans, so they are renumbered by the mean of `clustering.order_by` (clean cooking fuel share): 0 = lowest, 3 = highest of the four clusters by that measure | Keeps cluster ids stable across runs |
| 39 | A6 | Map boundaries are DataMeet's Census-2011 district polygons (641), simplified to about 1 km and snapped to a 0.001 degree grid for small files; borders are not survey-accurate. State polygons are the merged districts. No base map or tiles are used, so only these polygons are drawn | DataMeet is community-maintained and says it contains errors; the external boundary derives from a 2014 shapefile in their repository. If a map will be published, check the depiction of India's borders against Survey of India guidance |
| 40 | A6 | A district is drawn only if its name matches a polygon within the same state (normalised names, plus `config/boundary_name_map.csv` for 23 spelling variants such as Chamarajanagar/Chamrajnagar). Districts created after 2011 are NOT matched to a parent polygon and are not drawn: 635 of 706 NFHS-5 districts (about 5% of estimated population) appear, the rest are listed in `map_match_report.csv`. 6 polygons are grey (no data) | Guessing which old district a new one came from would show wrong values. Telangana (22 not drawn) is the main gap |
| 41 | A6 | The 2011 file puts Telangana's ten old districts under Andhra Pradesh and Ladakh under Jammu & Kashmir; `config/boundary_state_fix.csv` assigns their current state codes. The unnamed "Data Not Available" polygon in Jammu & Kashmir is kept in that state with no data | Matching is by state code, so the old state boundaries would otherwise break the join |
| 42 | A6 | Map metrics repeat what is in the tables: spend maps are state-level (uniform inside a state apart from urban share), and opportunity maps show the relative score of the district's cluster, not a district estimate. State maps aggregate ALL districts of a state, including those not drawn on the district map. Cluster colours (0 to 3) are categorical, though the clusters form a rough development gradient | Avoids implying more precision than the data has |
| 43 | A3b | HCES **quantity** is only recorded for rice, atta (and maida, suji), edible oils and ghee, spices, tea leaf and namkeen/bhujia (item 292). Biscuits, noodles, chips and the three personal-care categories have value only; tea cups are counted in numbers. Those categories are absent from `hces_unit_values.csv` | Checked in the unit-level files: the quantity column is empty for them |
| 44 | A3b | Units follow the data, converted to g or ml in `config/hces_item_map.csv` (`qty_factor`): rice, atta, ghee and namkeen in kg, oils in litres, spices and tea leaf in grams. Checked by implied prices (rice about Rs 38/kg, mustard oil about Rs 167/l, ghee about Rs 610/kg). Ghee is treated as ml with oils, ignoring density (about 0.91) | The Vol II questionnaire PDF was not parsed for units; the price check is the evidence |
| 45 | A3b | Monthly household quantity = sum over items of quantity x 30 / recall days, among buying households only; `buyer_share` is weighted buyers over weighted households in the state x sector. Median monthly quantity per household and per person is used as a **proxy for the pack size a household can use**: it says what a household consumes, not what pack it buys. Unit price paid = sum(value) / sum(quantity) | There is no purchase-occasion data, so pack size itself cannot be observed |
| 46 | A3c | OSM shop density (Geofabrik India extract, ODbL) is a **relative** signal of kirana presence, not a census. Kirana-like = `convenience, general, kiosk, grocery, variety_store`; modern trade = `supermarket, department_store`. Median district has only 2 mapped kirana-like shops and the top districts are large urban ones, so mapping effort, not shop counts, drives much of the variation | OSM coverage in India is uneven and skews urban; density is used as a rank-based feature, never as an absolute count |
| 47 | A6 | Boundaries now come from the LGD district layer (785 polygons, districts up to 2023, GeoParquet copy from the ramSeraph/indian_admin_boundaries release `districts`). It supersedes rows 39-41: all 706 NFHS-5 districts are drawn (was 635), Telangana and Ladakh use current state codes natively, and the DataMeet 2011 loader is kept as `source="datameet2011"`. 79 polygons are grey: districts created after the NFHS-5 survey (2019-21) | The upstream repository declares no licence, so the file is downloaded, never committed. Matching still uses names within the state, with 101 reviewed spellings in `config/boundary_name_map_lgd.csv` |
| 48 | A6 | NFHS-5 values for a district that was split after the survey (for example Visakhapatnam, Bellary, Darjiling) are drawn on today's smaller polygon, and the new child districts are grey. The values describe the old, larger district | Splitting data between parent and child would be a guess; a later step may inherit parent values for scoring and label them as inherited |
| 49 | A6 | Name renames are matched as the same district (Allahabad = Prayagraj, Gulbarga = Kalaburagi, Warangal Urban = Hanumakonda, Warangal Rural = Warangal, Sikkim North/West/South/East = Mangan/Gyalshing/Namchi/Gangtok). The Mirpur and Muzaffarabad polygons (J&K, no LGD code) are kept without data | Border depiction follows the LGD source and has not been checked against Survey of India guidance; check before publishing a map |
| 50 | A7 | District demand is monthly category spend per person x estimated population. Spend per person is the state-level HCES figure blended by each district's urban share, so districts in one state differ only through urban share and population. Score is the percentile rank of demand within the category | The HCES sample is not designed for district estimates; district-level spend stays exploratory in `*_district` columns |
| 51 | A7 | A pack should cost about a quarter of a household's monthly spend in the category (`scoring.pack_cover_fraction`, roughly one week), with 4.4 people per household (`scoring.household_size`). The category pack is the standard size whose price is closest in log terms. Standard sizes are priced at the category's median unit price so the ladder rises with size | Both numbers are modelling choices, not measurements; changing them moves recommendations, so they live in config. Oils and ghee share one category, so the unit price is dominated by oils |
| 52 | A7 | A brand's pack "fits" a district when its price is within 1.6x of the target price either way. Noodles, detergent, shampoo and personal wash mostly do not fit: BigBasket lists no sachet-size packs (Rs 1-10), while HCES spend implies a target of about Rs 10 | This is a catalogue gap, not a model error, and is the most direct signal of unserved low-price packs. It also means the BigBasket catalogue under-represents small packs sold in kirana stores |
| 53 | A8 | Questions are answered by tools over the warehouse (`pack_plan`, `explain_district`, `list_options`), never by free SQL or the model's memory. The default backend is plain string matching ("rules", no model). An optional local model through Ollama (default `qwen2.5:7b`) chooses the tool; no paid API is used | The model only sees tool results, so it cannot state a pack or price the warehouse did not return. The hosted site uses the rules backend because a local model cannot be hosted for free |
| 54 | A8 | `config/eval_questions.csv` has 30 questions (24 easy, 6 hard). Rules pass all easy questions and 3 of 6 hard ones (synonyms, regions such as "the north-east", typos and "top N in the south" are out of reach for string matching). The model backend is scored on the same file with `make eval BACKEND=ollama` | Passing means the first tool call and its arguments equal the expected ones; answer wording is not scored. User state names are mapped to the warehouse's NFHS spellings (Maharashtra to Maharastra, Delhi to NCT of Delhi) |
